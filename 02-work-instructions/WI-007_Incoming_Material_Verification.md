# WI-007 Incoming Material Verification

## 1. Purpose

Define the step-by-step process for receiving, verifying, and accepting incoming raw material to ensure it meets purchase order specifications and material certification requirements before being released to production.

## 2. Scope

Applies to all raw material received at the shop including:
- Bar stock (round, hex, flat, square)
- Plate and sheet stock
- Castings and forgings received for machining
- Pre-cut blanks from material suppliers
- Material from any new or existing supplier

Does NOT apply to tooling, consumables (coolant, inserts), or finished purchased components (those follow receiving inspection under QP-004 but do not require material certification review).

## 3. Required Tools and Equipment

- Digital calipers (for dimensional verification of stock size)
- Tape measure (for length verification)
- Material certification (mill cert / test report) — should arrive with shipment or via email
- Purchase Order copy (for matching against delivery)
- QF-003 Receiving Inspection Record (blank)
- Material alloy identification chart (for visual reference)
- Permanent marker or metal tags (for material identification)
- PMI gun (Positive Material Identification — if available and required by customer)
- Scale or weighing equipment (for quantity verification by weight if applicable)

## 4. Safety Precautions

- Wear gloves when handling raw material — edges of bar stock and plate can be sharp.
- Use proper lifting techniques or equipment for heavy stock (anything over 50 lbs).
- Inspect packaging for damage that may indicate material was compromised during shipping (bent, crushed, contaminated).
- Some materials arrive with oil or rust preventive coatings — avoid skin contact if sensitive.

## 5. Procedure

### Initial Receiving

1. Verify the delivery matches the Purchase Order (PO): supplier name, PO number, material description, quantity, and size.
2. Inspect packaging condition. Note any damage on the delivery receipt and on QF-003. Photograph significant damage.
3. Verify quantity received matches PO quantity. Count pieces or verify by weight if bulk material.
4. Check for material certification (mill cert or test report). The cert may be:
   - Included in the shipping package (paper copy)
   - Sent separately via email (check with purchasing if not present)
   - Available on the supplier's portal
5. If no cert is received with the shipment, place material on HOLD (yellow tag) and notify Purchasing to obtain the cert. Do NOT release to production without certification.

### Material Certification Review

6. Verify the cert matches the PO requirements:
   - **Alloy/Grade:** Cert alloy designation matches PO (e.g., 6061-T6, 303, 4140, etc.)
   - **Size/Form:** Cert dimensions match what was ordered
   - **Heat/Lot Number:** A unique heat or lot number is present on the cert
   - **Chemistry:** Chemical composition falls within the specification limits for the ordered alloy (compare cert values to spec limits in the alloy reference chart)
   - **Mechanical Properties:** Tensile strength, yield strength, hardness, and elongation meet the specification minimums (if required by the material specification)
   - **Condition/Temper:** Matches PO (e.g., T6, annealed, cold-drawn, hot-rolled)
7. Verify the cert is traceable — it must reference the heat/lot number that can be traced to the specific material received.
8. If the cert has ANY discrepancy (wrong alloy, values out of spec, missing heat number, wrong size), REJECT the material. Proceed to Nonconformance Response (Section 7).

### Visual and Dimensional Inspection

9. Visually inspect the material for:
   - Surface damage (gouges, deep scratches, pitting)
   - Contamination (rust beyond normal mill scale, chemical staining, foreign material)
   - Bending or warping beyond usable limits
   - Correct form (round vs hex, plate vs bar — matches PO)
10. Measure a representative sample of the stock:
    - Diameter or cross-section dimensions (minimum 3 locations along length)
    - Length (verify meets or exceeds PO requirement)
11. Verify measurements are within standard mill tolerances for the ordered size (e.g., ground stock within ±0.0005", cold-drawn within ±0.003", hot-rolled within standard ASTM tolerances).

### Identification and Acceptance

12. If all checks pass (cert matches, visual acceptable, dimensions correct):
    - Mark the acceptance on QF-003 Receiving Inspection Record. Sign and date.
    - Apply material identification to the stock: write the alloy, heat/lot number, and PO number directly on the material with permanent marker (or attach a metal tag for small pieces).
    - Move the material to the approved raw material storage area.
    - File the cert with the QF-003 record for traceability.
13. Enter the material into the material inventory log (alloy, size, heat/lot number, quantity, location, date received, PO number).

## 6. Acceptance Criteria

- Material certification present and traceable to the specific material received
- Alloy, condition, and size match Purchase Order
- Chemistry and mechanical properties within specification limits (per cert)
- Heat/lot number present and recorded
- No visual damage or contamination that would prevent use
- Dimensions within standard mill tolerances for the ordered size

## 7. Nonconformance Response

If material does not meet acceptance criteria:

1. **STOP** — do not release material to production.
2. Apply a red rejection tag to the material. Segregate in the Quarantine Area.
3. Initiate QF-011 Nonconforming Material Report per QP-008.
4. Notify Purchasing of the rejection and reason. Purchasing will coordinate with the supplier for:
   - Return/replacement
   - Supplier corrective action (if repeat issue)
5. Record the rejection on QF-003.
6. If material arrived damaged, file a freight claim with the carrier (photograph damage as evidence).
7. If using PMI and the reading does not match the cert, reject immediately — possible material mix-up is a critical safety issue for aerospace/defense parts.
