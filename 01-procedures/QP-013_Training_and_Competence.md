# QP-013 Training and Competence

## 1. Purpose

Define the process for identifying competence requirements, providing training, assessing effectiveness, maintaining training records, and managing organizational knowledge.

## 2. Scope

Applies to the Shop Owner (performing all production and quality roles) and any contracted personnel performing work affecting product quality (calibration lab, auditor, special process suppliers).

## 3. Responsibilities

| Role | Responsibility |
|------|---------------|
| Shop Owner | Identify competence requirements, obtain and maintain training, assess own competence, maintain training records, manage organizational knowledge |
| Contracted Services | Maintain their own qualifications and provide evidence of competence when requested |

## 4. References

- QF-016 - Training Record
- QF-036 - Training Matrix
- QP-011 - Internal Audit
- QP-012 - Management Review
- AS9100 Rev D Clauses 7.1.6, 7.2, 7.3

## 5. Definitions

- **Competence:** The demonstrated ability to apply knowledge and skills to achieve intended results.
- **Organizational Knowledge:** Knowledge specific to this operation that is necessary for the operation of processes and conformity of products (includes lessons learned, process know-how, tribal knowledge).
- **Training Matrix:** A document mapping required competencies to roles and tracking current qualification status.

## 6. Procedure

### 6.1 Competence Requirements

1. Maintain QF-036 Training Matrix identifying required competencies for each function performed:
   - CNC lathe operation and programming
   - Blueprint reading and GD&T interpretation
   - Measurement and inspection techniques
   - Quality system procedures (this QMS)
   - Material identification and handling
   - Safety (machine operation, hazardous materials)
   - Customer-specific requirements (where applicable)
2. Review competence requirements when processes change, new equipment is acquired, new customer requirements are received, or nonconformances indicate a training gap.

### 6.2 Training Methods

3. Acceptable training methods include:
   - Formal courses (external providers, online, manufacturer training)
   - Self-study with documented assessment
   - On-the-job experience (documented with dates and scope)
   - Industry conferences and seminars
   - Manufacturer equipment training
4. Record all training on QF-016 Training Record including: date, topic, provider/method, duration, and evidence of completion (certificate, test score, or self-assessment notation).

### 6.3 Effectiveness Assessment

5. Assess training effectiveness by one or more methods:
   - Demonstrated ability to perform the task correctly
   - Reduction in errors or nonconformances related to the trained topic
   - Successful completion of test or practical evaluation
   - Satisfactory audit results in the trained area
6. If training is assessed as ineffective, identify the gap and provide additional training or alternative methods.

### 6.4 Sole-Proprietor Competence Demonstration

7. As a single-operator LLC, continued competence is demonstrated through objective evidence including: successful production of conforming product (inspection records), positive customer feedback and repeat orders, maintenance of current certifications, results of external assessments (e.g., registrar audit findings related to competence), and self-assessment against the Training Matrix at each management review.

### 6.4 Ongoing Training Requirements

7. Annual refresher training is required for:
   - Quality system awareness (review of QMS changes)
   - Safety procedures
   - Customer-specific requirements (if changed)
   - FOD and counterfeit parts awareness
8. Additional training is triggered by:
   - New equipment acquisition
   - New manufacturing processes
   - New customer or industry requirements
   - Corrective actions identifying training as root cause
   - Changes to procedures or work instructions
9. Document all training events on QF-016 regardless of type (formal, informal, refresher).

### 6.5 New Personnel (Contracted Workers)

10. Before any contracted worker performs work affecting product quality, verify and record their qualifications.
11. Contracted calibration labs must provide ISO/IEC 17025 accreditation evidence.
12. Contracted auditors must provide auditor qualification evidence per QP-011.

### 6.6 Organizational Knowledge Management

13. Organizational knowledge for this operation includes:
    - Process parameters and setup notes captured in Work Instructions
    - Lessons learned documented in Corrective Action Records (QP-009)
    - Customer-specific requirements and preferences
    - Supplier capabilities and limitations
    - Equipment-specific techniques and workarounds
14. Organizational knowledge is captured through:
    - Work Instructions (WI series) for process methods
    - CAR records documenting root causes and solutions (preventive knowledge)
    - Notes fields in job records for customer preferences
15. Organizational knowledge is maintained and accessible by:
    - Storing all documents in the controlled QMS file structure
    - Reviewing and updating Work Instructions when processes change
    - Reviewing CAR trends at Management Review
16. New organizational knowledge needs are identified through:
    - New processes or equipment requiring WI development
    - Nonconformance trends indicating undocumented knowledge
    - Customer feedback indicating gaps
    - Management Review identifying training or documentation needs
